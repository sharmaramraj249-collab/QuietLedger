# Quiet Ledger — product proposal

## Three original concepts considered

| Concept | Pitch | Privacy value | Gemini boundary | Visual direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet Ledger | A worker proves they are eligible to register one anonymous workplace signal in a listening window. | Identity, credential, secret and raw response remain local; only a nullifier and aggregate count are public. | Explains only the public policy. | Japanese editorial calm. |
| Payslip Split | A team member proves a private pay-split agreement was followed without publishing individual compensation. | Salaries and split inputs remain private; settlement condition is public. | Converts public plan wording into a verifier checklist. | Neo-industrial financial terminal. |
| Doorframe | A resident proves a private eligibility condition to access a public service slot. | Age, address and credential stay private; approval is public. | Explains a public eligibility rule. | Scientific field instrument. |

## Selection

Quiet Ledger wins because privacy is central: a workplace signal is unsafe if a manager can connect it to a worker. It demonstrates in a minute, has an achievable first contract, and makes the worker—not the organization—the primary user. It is distinct from credential-passport and sustainability-verification products: it is a time-bounded, anonymous listening mechanism with replay prevention.

## Target users and problem

Workers need a credible way to signal conditions without trading safety for participation. Organizations need trustworthy aggregate evidence without building an identity-bearing feedback database. Midnight validates membership and uniqueness without making the worker legible.
